ESA Housing Guidelines in Hawaii Under the Fair Housing Act (FHA), eligibility for an emotional support animal requires a qualifying mental or emotional condition that substantially limits one or more major life activities. This federal law protects individuals from housing discrimination and allows them to request reasonable accommodations for an ESA when supported by appropriate documentation from a licensed mental health professional. Federal Housing Guidelines  Case-by-Case Evaluation Requirement Housing providers are required to review every ESA request individually, considering the person’s specific situation instead of applying blanket pet rules or breed-based restrictions.  Undue Hardship Consideration A request may be declined if it results in significant difficulty or expense for the housing provider, but such decisions must be supported with clear and valid justification.  Direct Threat Assessment Criteria An ESA can only be denied if there is credible, evidence-based proof that the animal poses a real risk to health or safety and not assumptions or generalized beliefs. Housing Provider Roles & Responsibilities  Verification of Valid Documentation Landlords are allowed to request credible documentation from a licensed professional, but they cannot ask for detailed medical records or intrusive personal health information.  Enforcement of Property Rules Housing providers may apply reasonable policies regarding cleanliness, noise, and safety, provided these rules are enforced fairly and do not single out ESA owners.  Responsibility for Property Damage Tenants are still accountable for any actual damage caused by their ESA, even though landlords cannot charge standard pet fees or deposits in advance.  Important Legal Notice The Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 recognizes emotional support animals only in the context of housing-related accommodations.
